FIELD NOTES / 02

Inside a steam turbine.

Steam expands through fixed guide vanes and rotating blades, transferring energy to the shaft. The coupled generator converts shaft power to electricity. This is a generic, simplified axial turbine—not a particular manufacturer's machine.

Explore: drag to orbit, scroll or pinch to zoom, and click a cycle card or a component. Cutaway removes the upper casing; Exploded raises it along with the upper guide vanes and generator cover. The remaining rows continue to rotate. Peripheral boiler, condenser, and pump geometry is schematic and not to the same scale as the turbine.

Keyboard: 1 assembled · 2 cutaway · 3 exploded · Space start/stop · T orbit tour · R reset view. The idle tour starts after 12 seconds; turn Orbit tour off to disable it. Setting animation speed to zero freezes motion, while telemetry continues.

Physics model

Internal flow: hot vapor is invisible. The colored streaks and arrows are explanatory overlays, not a rendering of visible hot steam. Soft mist represents suspended droplets only when the approximate exhaust quality is below 100%. No steam is vented from the sealed machine. Cutaway and exploded views are inspection conventions; the real turbine needs its casing closed to operate.

Inspect stage: an enlarged, open sector of fixed silver guide vanes directs flow toward gold rotor blades. The cyan arrow represents tangential force, not another steam stream. The shaft keeps rotating at the common animation scale; inspection temporarily slows it. Stage selection changes the representative blade size and estimated boundary readings. Pressure is distributed geometrically between inlet and exhaust; enthalpy and temperature are interpolated across six stages. These distributions, streamlines and blade profiles are illustrative, not CFD or manufacturing geometry.

Mechanism references: DOE steam turbine training and GE Vernova: how steam turbines work.

The display uses a quasi-steady Rankine-cycle approximation, not a validated steam-table solver. Inlet enthalpy and entropy are fitted classroom estimates with cₚ = 2.3 kJ/(kg·K) and R = 0.4615 kJ/(kg·K). Low-pressure saturation values use rounded anchors at 0.05–1 bar with log-pressure interpolation. Wet and superheated exhaust regions are handled separately.

h₃ = 2800 + 2.3(Tin − 250) kJ/kg
s₃ = 6.1 + 2.3 ln[(Tin + 273.15)/523.15] − 0.4615 ln(Pin/40)
x₄s = (s₃ − sf)/sfg; h₄s = hf + x₄s hfg in the wet region
h₄ = h₃ − ηt(h₃ − h₄s), with ηt = 82%
Wt = ṁ(h₃ − h₄); shaft efficiency = 98%, generator efficiency = 96%
wp = v(Pin − Pout)/ηp, v = 0.001 m³/kg, ηp = 75%, pump motor efficiency = 90%
Qin = ṁ(h₃ − h₂); Qout = ṁ(h₄ − h₁)
Qin = Pnet + Qout + mechanical/electrical losses

The governor targets 3,000 rpm for a two-pole, nominal 50 Hz generator. Start/stop is a smooth illustrative ramp, not a grid-synchronization or rotor-dynamics model. Torque is derived from shaft power divided by angular speed. Blade geometry, stage pressure distribution, and particle speed are illustrative. During operation, telemetry updates from the same state that drives shaft rotation.
